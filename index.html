Approach

Methods first,
conclusions second.

Most of this portfolio started with a question a business would actually pay to answer: does a marquee signing pay for itself? Does home field survive an empty stadium? Does initiating a dividend move the stock? The method follows the question, not the other way around.

That means event-study design borrowed from financial economics, difference-in-differences with parallel-trends checks, two-proportion z-tests validated against statsmodels, leave-one-out cross-validation hand-implemented in base R, and composite risk scoring rendered on an interactive global map.

It also means publishing the results that didn't work. Three of these projects return statistically insignificant findings — and they say so, in the headline. A portfolio that only contains wins isn't a portfolio, it's a highlight reel.

Supply-Chain Contagion Model — Phase 1

Phase 1 of a credit-risk pipeline that extracts disclosed major-customer concentration statements from 10-K filings and resolves each named customer to a ticker/CIK. Costco Comp Prediction Ledger

A falsifiable-prediction ledger for Costco's monthly comparable sales. Two models forecast reported total-company comp before each release — a deliberately naive trailing three-month average as champion, and a challenger adding gas and FX terms (+0.0984 × gas YoY, −0.2302 × FX basket YoY) — and each prediction is committed to git so the commit timestamp is the evidence. The protocol was locked in a pre-registration document before any month was scored and is never edited; changes ship as separate, individually timestamped amendments.
